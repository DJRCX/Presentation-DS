4. Look for relationships

This is probably the most important part of your ER diagram.

Ask:

"How are these entities connected?"

For example:

Hospital → Department

One hospital can have many departments.

Hospital 1 ─────────── N Department

So:

Hospital HAS Departments

Department → Doctor

A doctor may work in multiple departments, and a department can have multiple doctors.

Therefore:

Doctor M ─────────── N Department

That's a many-to-many relationship.

You cannot directly implement this easily in a relational database, so you create an associative entity:

Doctor_Department
------------------
Doctor_ID (PK, FK)
Department_ID (PK, FK)
Joining_Date
Position

Now:

Doctor 1 ─── N Doctor_Department N ─── 1 Department

What each member should actually be responsible for

I'd give everyone a specific section of the database, not just a random set of slides:

Member 1 — System Analyst

"What does the healthcare system need to manage?"

Member 2 — Hospital & Staff

"How are hospitals, departments and doctors connected?"

Member 3 — Patient Management

"How do patients interact with doctors and healthcare services?"

Member 4 — Services & Support

"How are prescriptions, medication, insurance and ambulance services modelled?"

Member 5 — Database Systemer

"How do all these relationships become an ER diagram and relational schema?"

Problem → Entities → Relationships → Services → Database
